How should financial advisers nurture prospects who are not ready to proceed?

Lead conversion in specific industries 4 min read · Published 20 September 2026 · Updated 20 September 2026

Ask what the person is waiting for and agree on when you will contact them again. If they want information in the meantime, send general material related to the decision they are making. Keep personal advice inside the firm's authorised advice process. Be available when their timing changes without filling their inbox or using market anxiety to make them book.

Not ready can mean many things. A person may be waiting for a business sale, inheritance, retirement date, property transaction, vesting event or simply the confidence to organise their finances. If the firm does not record that timing, it will either contact them too often or forget them. Good long-term follow-up is built around the event they named.

Not ready often means later

Find out whether the delay is about timing, whether the service is right for them or whether the firm can help at all. Ask what needs to happen before they are ready, how they want to hear from you and whether a date or event should prompt the next call. The firm should be clear about which messages are general information, marketing or financial advice, with compliance approving the boundaries.

  • Prospects are marked lost when they name a future event.

  • Newsletter content is unrelated to the reason they enquired.

  • Advisers keep private reminder lists outside the CRM.

  • Messages imply personalised advice before the advice process.

  • Automation continues after the person opts out or becomes a client.

Follow the timing they gave you

Base future contact on the person's timing and questions, not assumptions about their wealth or fears. You might agree to call on a certain date, send a general guide or invite them to an educational event. If they reply with new information about their circumstances, a qualified person should review it. Do not use automation to make personal recommendations outside the approved advice process.

1. Record the prospect's stated goal, timing trigger, channel preference and consent.

2. Classify content under the firm's compliance framework before use.

3. Schedule the agreed human check-in and use relevant general information sparingly.

4. Route replies and changed circumstances to an authorised person.

5. Review inactive records, suppression status and data retention under approved policy.

What to track

Check how many people reply when the agreed date arrives, attend a consultation and decide to proceed. Track opt-outs as well. Email clicks alone do not tell you whether the contact was helpful. Staff should be able to explain why each person is receiving a message. Have legal and compliance advisors approve the process under current financial services, privacy and marketing rules.

What to avoid

  • Using market fear or deadline pressure to manufacture urgency.

  • Presenting general content as if it were personal advice.

  • Contacting people on arbitrary dates instead of their stated trigger.

  • Keeping sensitive notes in uncontrolled personal systems.

  • Assuming a high-value prospect wants frequent communication.

Common questions

How often should advisers contact a future prospect?

Use the timing you agreed and send information only when it relates to the person's plans. A few helpful contacts can be better than a monthly email that ignores what they said.

Can AI create nurture content?

It can help draft material under the firm's controls, but qualified people must check the facts, claims, approvals and whether the content crosses into advice.

Should every not-ready prospect receive ongoing emails?

No. Confirm that the person is suitable, interested and has agreed to hear from the firm. Some people prefer to return when they are ready, and the firm should respect that.

A realistic example

Example: A person expects to seek advice after selling a business in six months. The adviser records the event and permission, sends one approved preparation guide and schedules a call near the expected date. No product recommendation is sent in the meantime. This example is general information and not financial advice.

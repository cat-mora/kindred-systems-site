How can a business improve its chances of being recommended by ChatGPT?

Answer Engine Optimisation 4 min read · Published 20 September 2026 · Updated 20 September 2026

Make it easy for ChatGPT and other answer engines to understand what your business does and confirm that the information is accurate. Publish clear answers to real client questions, name the author and keep your services, locations and business details consistent across the web. Make sure relevant crawlers can access the site. Nobody can guarantee a recommendation, ranking or citation.

ChatGPT recommendations are not a directory listing a business can simply claim. Answers may draw on web search, indexed pages and other sources, and the exact sources can vary by question, location and time. The practical job is to make useful facts clear, accessible and supported. That work overlaps with search optimisation, but it also rewards direct answers, strong entity information and evidence that another system can check.

What answer engines need to understand

Start with the questions people ask before choosing a provider. Does your website answer them clearly? Can a search crawler tell what you do, who you help, where you work, who wrote the content and when it was updated? Check that service names, contact details and claims are consistent across your website, profiles and industry listings. Important information should not be trapped inside images or blocked from crawlers.

  • Service pages rely on slogans and never state the problem solved.

  • Business details differ across the website, directories and profiles.

  • Articles have no author, date, sources or clear answer near the top.

  • Important pages block search crawlers unintentionally.

  • The business publishes large volumes of generic AI-written content with little first-hand value.

Strengthen the information about your business

Build a useful collection of pages around questions clients ask. Give each page one main question, answer it near the top and use headings that explain what follows. Include examples and link to reliable sources for factual claims. Your service, about and contact pages should also say clearly who the business is and what it offers. Use structured data only when it agrees with what people can see on the page, and never invent reviews, awards or mentions.

1. Research public question patterns, client conversations and search data without inventing private ChatGPT volumes.

2. Audit crawl access, indexing, canonical URLs, page titles, internal links and mobile usability.

3. Publish useful question-led pages with accountable authorship, dates and sources.

4. Keep business identity, services, locations and expertise consistent on owned and credible third-party pages.

5. Measure indexed pages, citations, referral visits and qualified enquiries, then update content as facts and questions change.

What to track

Track which pages are indexed, search visibility, visits from AI services where your analytics can identify them, enquiries and whether your business appears for a fixed set of test questions. Results can change with the wording, the user and model updates, so a test is only a snapshot. The important question is whether the right people are finding accurate information and contacting the business.

What can reduce your chances

  • Promising a guaranteed position or recommendation in ChatGPT.

  • Creating thin pages for hundreds of near-identical questions.

  • Adding schema that does not match the visible page.

  • Blocking relevant crawlers while expecting discovery.

  • Publishing unsupported awards, results, reviews or expertise claims.

Common questions

Is AEO the same as SEO?

They overlap. Technical access, clear content, authority and reputation help both. AEO places extra emphasis on direct answers, verifiable facts, entity clarity and whether an answer system can extract and cite the page.

Does schema guarantee a citation?

No. Structured data can help machines interpret eligible content, but it does not guarantee indexing, display or recommendation.

A realistic example

Example: A specialist service firm replaces vague industry pages with clear service descriptions and publishes accountable answers to common buying questions. It corrects inconsistent profile details, checks crawler access and tracks a fixed set of prompts over time. The example describes a process, not a guarantee of visibility or recommendation.

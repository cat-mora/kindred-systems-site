Every day, people ask AI tools to recommend businesses. "What's the best accountant in Brisbane?" "Which project management software is worth paying for?" "Who does commercial cleaning in Melbourne CBD?" AI answers those questions, and it recommends specific businesses.

Here's the problem. These tools don't browse the internet the way a person does. They crawl your site using AI agents that strip out images, ignore JavaScript, and look for very specific signals to decide if your content is trustworthy and citable. Most websites were built for human eyes. They fail those checks without anyone realising.

This is not a future problem. It is a right-now problem. Most of your competitors don't know this yet either, which means there's still a window to get ahead of it.

What we look at

What the audit checks.

These are the factors that determine whether AI agents can find, read and cite your business. They are not the same factors as traditional SEO.

AI crawler access

Whether robots.txt is accidentally blocking AI crawlers like GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot. If AI can't crawl your site, it can't recommend you.

llms.txt presence

A newer file format that tells AI agents exactly what your business does and what content on your site matters most. Most sites still don't have one.

JavaScript rendering

46% of AI crawler visits strip out all CSS, JavaScript and images. If your key content only loads via JavaScript, AI agents may not be reading it.

Schema markup

Structured data, especially FAQ schema, helps AI understand what questions your content answers. Sites with good schema are more likely to be cited directly.

Content structure

Whether headings, paragraphs and answers are written in a way AI agents can extract and quote. Direct answers to real questions score well.

Page token efficiency

AI processes pages within a token limit. If pages are bloated with navigation, footers or repeated boilerplate, the substantive content may not get read.

E-E-A-T signals

Author credentials, citations and real-world experience signals. AI tools are trained to prefer sources that demonstrate genuine expertise.

Site speed and crawlability

Pages that load slowly or have broken internal links create problems for any crawler, including AI. We check for the basics that block access.

Server-side rendering

Whether your core content is visible in the raw HTML, or depends on the browser to build the page. AI agents often see raw HTML only.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions.

I already have good SEO. Doesn't that mean AI can find me?

Not necessarily. SEO optimises for search engine ranking algorithms. AEO (answer engine optimisation) optimises for AI agent comprehension. The signals are different. A site can rank well on Google and still be largely invisible to AI tools, because they use different crawlers, different reading logic, and different criteria for what counts as a trustworthy source.

Which AI tools does the audit cover?

The audit checks factors that affect visibility across the major AI tools, including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Claude. The underlying signals, crawler access, content structure, schema and E-E-A-T, affect all of them, since they use similar crawling and comprehension logic.

Is my site definitely invisible to AI right now?

We don't know until we check. Some sites have most of the basics right. Others have a single technical setting, like a misconfigured robots.txt file, that blocks everything. The audit tells you exactly where your site sits. Many businesses are surprised in both directions.

What if I don't have a technical person to act on the recommendations?

The report tells you what needs fixing and why, in plain language. Some fixes are things you or your marketing manager can do directly, such as content changes or adding an FAQ section. Others require a developer, and the report notes which is which. If you want help implementing the fixes, get in touch and we can talk about what that looks like.

How long does the audit take?

Do you audit any type of business website?

Yes. The audit checks apply regardless of industry. The one requirement is a live website that can be accessed publicly.
